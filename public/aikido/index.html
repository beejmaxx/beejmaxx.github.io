Technical overview

Aikido

Aikido is a private research and execution codebase for evaluating trading policies against historical point-in-time evidence and realizing approved policies through a shared runtime model.

Purpose

The system treats policy as the active decision object. A policy maps observer state, current exposure, and safety state to requested target exposure. Replay and live execution are expected to share the same accounting, legality, and session semantics.

The system is organized to answer four separate questions:

  1. What market and research evidence exists?
  2. What information was available at a decision boundary?
  3. What exposure did the policy request?
  4. What did the runtime actually realize, and how should that result be judged?

Research may query broad warehouse state. Policy and runtime should consume a frozen publication rather than rebuilding point-in-time state or reading mutable warehouse views in the decision loop.

Non-negotiable behavior

  • Holdout data measures frozen decisions and does not feed those decisions.
  • Evidence requires at least 126 out-of-sample trading days; 189 is the target.
  • Replay is flat at 16:00 ET and does not carry overnight positions.
  • Market-bar and trade timestamps use America/New_York time.
  • Runtime and evaluation share account and execution semantics.
  • Requested, executable, and realized exposure remain distinguishable.
  • Promotion uses multiple gates rather than one performance score.

Current architecture status

The target boundaries are defined, but the implementation is still converging. Canonical research evidence belongs in discovery; research queues and operational boards belong in research_control; frozen policy-state packs are the downstream decision contract; runtime and evaluation own realized truth. Existing portfolio.* point-in-time surfaces are transitional compatibility layers rather than the target foundation.

See the architecture summary for the current-to-target mapping and ownership rules.
